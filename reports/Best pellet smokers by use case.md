# Searwood 600 leads nine pellet-smoker picks

For a US buyer on 17 November 2026, the Weber Searwood 600 is the pellet grill to buy unless you have a specific reason to want something else. At **$899 on early Black Friday pricing ($999 regular)** it wins both best overall and best for searing, because it is the only mid-price pellet grill that puts direct flame across the whole grate and has measured heat-up data behind it. The other seven categories go to the Pit Boss 850 DX for budget ($649 regular; today's price is unverified, and the Weber Smoque is the better buy while it stays at $599), the Grilla Mammoth vertical cabinet smoker for crowds, the Traeger Woodridge Pro for the app, the Yoder YS640S as the buy-once premium grill, and the Lone Star Grillz 20x36 for both smoke flavor and cold or windy climates; portable has no single winner and splits three ways by where you will cook. These picks came from four research files, a council of three different AI models that answered independently, and a judge that re-checked disputed facts on live pages. The council's near-unanimity is weaker than it looks, because every seat read the same notes and those notes lean heavily on one review outlet. **Overall confidence is 0.5 on a 0-to-1 scale**: the overall, searing, app and premium picks rest on current hands-on testing, while the cold-weather, smoke-flavor, portable and capacity picks rest on single reviewers, owner anecdotes or maker claims. Several of the best-known review outlets could not be read at all, and a short list of facts, starting with today's Pit Boss price, still needs a human check before you pay.

## The Searwood wins twice on measured heat, and the budget and app picks carry caveats

### Best overall: the one mid-price grill that both smokes and sears

The Searwood 600 is **Best Overall in two of the three current hands-on roundups the researchers could read**, Smoked BBQ Source's Fall 2026 guide and Taste of Home's September 2026 update ([Smoked BBQ Source](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/best-pellet-smokers/); [Taste of Home](https://www.tasteofhome.com/collection/best-pellet-grills/)). The third, Bob Vila, gave the searing award to its larger sibling and named the Traeger Westwood XL best overall, a grill that tops out at 450°F and needed a skillet to finish steaks ([Bob Vila](https://www.bobvila.com/reviews/best-pellet-grill/)). The Searwood's numbers are the best documented in this market: it reached **600°F in 13 minutes** and its controller never moved more than 5°F from the set point ([Smoked BBQ Source Searwood review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=57742)), and 1,820 Lowe's buyers rate it 4.6 out of 5 ([Lowe's reviews](https://www.lowes.com/reviews/weber-1500120-grills/5014658797)). The trade-offs are real. Two measurements found a **30 to 50°F spread across the grate**, so food needs rotating; the same tester called the wheels "a joke" and found the barbecue "a tad drier" than on rival grills ([Smoked BBQ Source top-five video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-ZPJ4veZVY)). Shelves cost extra, and nobody has more than about a year of durability evidence on it. The judge's check of Weber's warranty page found five years on the cookbox and three on electrical parts, grates and burn pot, with the owner's guide as the controlling document.

The runner-up is the Traeger Woodridge Pro, for buyers who never sear and want the best app. It loses the top spot because at a 500°F setting its grates measured under 300°F ([Smoked BBQ Source](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/best-pellet-smokers/)). Skip the Searwood if you smoke low and slow almost exclusively and want even heat without rotating food, if you need more than 420 square inches on the main grate (the XL 600 has 630), or if you want to start the grill from your phone, which Weber does not allow ([Weber](https://www.weber.com/US/en/wood-pellet/searwood/1500120.html)).

### Searing: the Searwood again, unless you want 800°F and up

The same grill takes the searing category for the same reason. Direct flame reaches the whole cooking surface, and the Smoked BBQ Source tester called its searing "the best that I've ever tested" ([Smoked BBQ Source top-five video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-ZPJ4veZVY)). Confidence drops to 0.55 because the one grate-temperature figure, **about 585°F at the 600°F setting, is unverified**: it comes from a search summary of an AmazingRibs review that nobody could open, and two owners report weak searing at 500°F ([SmokingMeatForums](https://www.smokingmeatforums.com/threads/no-sear-on-searwood.334946/latest)). That heat browns well but sits below steakhouse-broiler level, and the right side of the grate runs hotter than the left.

The runner-up is the recteq X-Fire Pro 825. The judge confirmed its **$1,549.99** price at four retailers; its 1,250°F maximum is the maker's figure and has no independent measurement. Engadget scored it 84 out of 100 and praised direct-flame finishing on steaks, while noting two small 10 lb hoppers, no smoke-boost mode and wider temperature swings on a cold, windy day ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/home/kitchen-tech/recteq-x-fire-pro-825-review-a-smart-grill-that-pulls-double-duty-170000586.html)). A buyer who wants a true steakhouse crust should take the recteq, or pair any pellet grill with a propane sear burner, and not buy the Searwood for that job.

### Budget: Pit Boss 850 DX on its test record, Weber Smoque while it is $599

The Pit Boss 850 DX is the standing budget pick because it qualifies at its regular **$649** and has the stronger test record for value. Smoked BBQ Source, the one outlet that tested both it and its rival, names it Best Budget and "the best entry-level pellet grill on the market today" ([Smoked BBQ Source](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/best-pellet-smokers/); [top-five video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-ZPJ4veZVY)). It has PID control, WiFi, 840 square inches and a 21 lb hopper, and it held steady through cooks of eight hours and more ([Smoked BBQ Source budget roundup](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=51027)). Its weaknesses in the same test are a left-to-right hot spot, a small sear zone and an app that took about 45 minutes to set up. Owners across the Pit Boss range also report controller and igniter faults ([PitBossForum](https://www.pitbossforum.com/threads/an-observation-on-pb-issues.3912)).

Two facts about it are open. It is sold only through Lowe's, and Lowe's hides today's price behind a store selector, so the judge could not confirm it; 2026 sale prices were $499 in July and $449 until 7 September. Pit Boss also advertises up to five years of warranty on "select" pellet grills, and no source confirms the 850 DX is one of them ([Pit Boss warranty](https://www.pitboss-grills.com/pages/warranty)).

The alternative is the Weber Smoque, **confirmed at $599 against a regular $799** ([Weber Smoque](https://amer.weber.com/en-us/products/weber-smoque-pellet-smoker-black-1500770)). It tested as the most even-heating grill in this price band and carries a five-year warranty, but it has no direct sear, lighter steel and "flimsy plastic wheels," and Smoked BBQ Source found its smoke "not as deep as we would want" ([Smoked BBQ Source Smoque review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/weber-smoque-review/); [budget roundup](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=51027)). It stops being a budget grill when the sale ends. Neither grill sears well, and the 850 DX is the wrong buy for anyone who will be annoyed by app friction.

### Smart and app: Traeger Woodridge Pro, with company risk attached

Traeger's app holds **4.8 stars from about 312,000 iOS ratings** and was updated within days of the research ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/traeger/id1094569463)), and Smoked BBQ Source called it "the benchmark for reliability and ease of use" in November 2026 ([Smoked BBQ Source](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/best-pellet-smokers/)). The 4.8 figure partly reflects in-app rating prompts; the stronger evidence is reviewer testimony and features, such as recipes that send their settings to the grill ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/best-smart-grills-and-how-to-choose-one-153006286.html)). The Woodridge Pro adds a pellet-level sensor and a Super Smoke mode to the base model ([Traeger Woodridge](https://www.traeger.com/woodridge)).

The risk is the company, not the software. Traeger's third-quarter 2026 revenue fell **40.4% to $74.7 million**, it carries about $400.7 million of notes payable, and it needed a 1-for-50 reverse stock split in March 2026 ([Traeger 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001857853/000162828026070127/q3-26earningspressrelease.htm)). The app depends on Traeger's cloud, and in 2020 Traeger dropped support for older controller firmware ([TraegerForum](https://www.traegerforum.com/threads/wifire-woes.1401)). Better Business Bureau complaints in 2026 include a Woodridge Pro control panel that would not power on ([BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/ut/salt-lake-cty/profile/barbecue/traeger-pellet-grills-llc-1166-22294645/complaints)). The research notes left the warranty in conflict, three years on one Traeger page against ten at retailers. The judge resolved it: Traeger's Woodridge pages state a **10-year limited warranty**, and a retailer breakdown gives ten years on the body against rust-through and three years on the controller, auger motor, auger and fan. The electronics an app buyer depends on therefore have three years of cover, not ten.

The runner-up is the Searwood 600. Weber Connect guides cooks well, but it has a far smaller rating base (about 2,800 iOS ratings), weaker graphs and no remote start ([Apple App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1480540695)). Do not buy the Woodridge Pro to sear, to log detailed cook data (the controllers on the Yoder and Lone Star Grillz do that better), or if you need local control that works without Traeger's servers.

## The premium pick holds up, while smoke, cold, capacity and portable rest on thinner evidence

### Premium, buy once: Yoder YS640S, narrowly

The YS640S costs **$2,699 plus shipping** ([Yoder 2026 price list](https://www.yodersmokers.com/newpricelist-2026/)). It has a 10-gauge (about 1/8 inch) steel cooking chamber, is made in Kansas, weighs 335 lb, and carries ten years of warranty on the chamber and three on the controller and igniter ([Yoder YS640S](https://yodersmokers.com/ys640-pellet-grill.html)). Smoked BBQ Source named it Best High-End in November 2026 ("buy once, cry once") ([Smoked BBQ Source](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/best-pellet-smokers/)), and its reviewer has used one for years with no failures or rust, though Yoder supplied the review unit free ([Smoked BBQ Source YS640s review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/yoder-ys640s-review/)). The main long-term risk is the finish: the warranty excludes paint, rust and oxidation unless the structure fails ([Yoder warranty](https://www.yodersmokers.com/support/pellet/Yoder-Smokers-Pellet-Grill-Warranty-Document.pdf)). There is also no pellet dump door. Do not buy it for a humid or coastal yard unless you will keep it covered and touch up the paint, or if you need to move the grill often.

The Lone Star Grillz 20x36 at $2,945 is the runner-up and is stronger on paper, with 3/16 inch steel and a lifetime warranty on the main chamber and structural steel ([Lone Star Grillz](https://lonestargrillz.com/products/pellet-smoker)). It loses on an 8 to 14 week wait and an older independent review, published in 2024 and last updated in May 2025 ([Smoked BBQ Source LSG review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/lone-star-grillz-pellet-grill-review/)). The judge calls premium and smoke flavor a two-horse race in which either grill is defensible for either category. MAK, the stainless alternative, is excluded because it announced its closure in 2026 and said nothing about parts or warranty ([CookOut News](https://www.cookoutnews.com/?p=12069)).

### Smoke flavor: Lone Star Grillz 20x36, on one reviewer's word

This pick rests on a single reviewer who wrote that on the Lone Star "the bark and smoke flavor is unrivaled," and who credits a burn pot that lets hot-burning pellets mix with smoldering ones ([Smoked BBQ Source LSG review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/lone-star-grillz-pellet-grill-review/)). That review covered the larger 20x42. The judge confirmed that Lone Star's 20x36 page describes the same burn-pot design, but no source compares the two sizes directly. Owners dispute the claim among themselves ([AmazingRibs forum](https://pitmaster.amazingribs.com/forum/pellet-cookers/lone-star-grillz-lsg/1843486-are-they-really-that-smoky)), and **no blind taste test comparing pellet grills exists**. The Yoder YS640S is a near tie: its reviewer found it "noticeably smokier than most pellet grills I've run" ([Smoked BBQ Source YS640s review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/yoder-ys640s-review/)). Under $1,000 the pick is the Searwood 600.

The Camp Chef Woodwind Pro, which burns wood chunks in a separate smoke box, would have been the mid-price pick. It is sold out on Camp Chef's site with a dealer-only note ([Camp Chef](https://www.campchef.com/collections/pellet-grills)) and one review site reports it discontinued, so it was excluded on availability. A buyer who finds dealer stock could still consider it, knowing parts support is uncertain. The Lone Star is wrong for anyone who needs a grill quickly or wants high-heat grilling, since its air temperature tops out at 450°F.

### Cold or windy climates: the weakest pick in the set

No controlled cold-weather test exists for any current pellet grill. Every claim here is an owner anecdote, mostly from 2022 and 2023 and often about older models. The Lone Star Grillz 20x36 wins on construction and on those anecdotes: a 3/16 inch steel chamber, gasketed doors and a 40 lb hopper ([Lone Star Grillz](https://lonestargrillz.com/products/pellet-smoker)); a reviewer in Wisconsin who says the larger model "retains heat and maintains temperatures, even in the dead of winter" ([Smoked BBQ Source LSG review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/lone-star-grillz-pellet-grill-review/)); and a Buffalo owner who reports "It holds the heat very well" at 20°F in high wind ([AmazingRibs forum](https://pitmaster.amazingribs.com/forum/pellet-cookers/lone-star-grillz-lsg/1460935-lsg-pellet-grill-in-winter)).

The case against is that thick steel steadies temperature but does not stop heat loss in wind. A Yoder owner in western Kansas reports in the same thread that pellet consumption "increases dramatically" in wind until a welding blanket goes on, and a Lone Star owner says the controller "gets a little glitchy in cold weather" ([AmazingRibs forum](https://pitmaster.amazingribs.com/forum/pellet-cookers/lone-star-grillz-lsg/1460935-lsg-pellet-grill-in-winter)). Owner reports agree that wind matters more than cold. All three council seats chose this grill at 0.3 to 0.35 confidence, and all three said **the value answer for most buyers is a competent PID grill, an insulated blanket and a windbreak**; owners running blanketed grills at about 25°F and about -4°F reported no control problems ([AmazingRibs forum](https://pitmaster.amazingribs.com/forum/pellet-cookers/other-pellet-cookers/1156411-pellet-smokers-winter-performance)). The judge's instruction is to read this pick as "best performer on thin evidence." The runner-up, the Traeger Ironwood at $1,799.99, has a fully insulated dual-wall body ([Traeger Ironwood](https://www.traeger.com/pellet-grills/ironwood)), but nobody has documented the current generation in cold weather and it carries Traeger's company risk. Most buyers should not spend $2,945 for this reason alone, and nobody who needs a grill within two months or expects high-heat cooking below freezing should.

### Large capacity: Grilla Mammoth, a smoker that cannot grill

The Mammoth is Smoked BBQ Source's Best Large pick and its top vertical smoker ([Smoked BBQ Source](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/best-pellet-smokers/)). The judge's check of Grilla's page found 1,660 square inches, a 180 to 450°F range, a 40 lb hopper, a four-year warranty and shipping in one to two business days. It is double-wall insulated, and in testing two pork butts cooked for 22 hours with **the hopper still about half full** ([Smoked BBQ Source vertical guide](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=24112)). It is a smoker only. It cannot sear or crisp poultry skin at high heat, its fan is loud, it has no handle, and loading wood chunks is awkward. No tester has published rack-to-rack temperature data for any vertical smoker, and every brisket or pork-butt count for the Mammoth, the Lone Star and the Yoder YS1500S is a maker or dealer claim.

The seats agreed on the Mammoth and differed only on the alternative, so the judge kept both. The Pit Boss Sportsman 5-Series vertical is the cheaper smoker-only option at $649.99, with 1,679 square inches and a 60 lb hopper ([Pit Boss](https://www.pitboss-grills.com/collections/vertical-smokers); [Smoked BBQ Source vertical guide](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=24112)). The Yoder YS1500S at $5,195 is the horizontal that also grills ([Yoder 2026 price list](https://www.yodersmokers.com/newpricelist-2026/)). A household that wants one grill for everything should not buy a vertical; the Searwood XL 600, with 630 square inches on its main grate at $1,199, is the compromise.

### Portable: three winners for three situations

No portable pellet grill wins across the board, and all of them are heavy. The only dedicated comparison, The Barbecue Lab's test of eight portables, chose the Traeger Ranger for camping and the Grilla Chimp 2.0 for tailgating ([Barbecue Lab video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g344XLBP3VI)). OutdoorGearLab measured the Ranger at **54 lb and 176 square inches**, scored it 9 out of 10 for wind resistance and 3 out of 10 for portability, and noted that it needs a 120V outlet ([OutdoorGearLab](https://www.outdoorgearlab.com/reviews/camping-and-hiking/portable-grill/traeger-ranger-pellet)). The Chimp 2.0 offers 460 square inches but weighs 90 lb and runs on 120V only. Seat B had declined to pick it because the notes did not confirm it was still sold; the judge found it on Grilla's site at $719.99 and $503.99, shipping in one to two business days, which removed the objection.

For cooking without mains power the pick is the GMG Trek Prime 2.0 at $349, which GMG says runs natively on 12V and draws at most 165 W ([GMG Trek](https://greenmountaingrills.com/?p=180635)). No test in the research exercised that 12V claim, and GMG's main app averages 2.6 stars from 487 reviews ([AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/green-mountain-grills/851653884?country=us)). Each unit has a buyer it does not suit: the Ranger is too small for a group, the Chimp 2.0 is a two-person lift that needs an outlet, and the Trek is a poor choice for anyone who cares about the app. The Traeger Tailgater ($499.99) is the lighter, easy-to-find tailgating fallback. It wins most general roundups by default but tested poorly: the lowest cooking score on Bob Vila's list, a rusted tray in Taste of Home's long-term use, and three ash fires for one Outdoor Life writer ([Bob Vila](https://www.bobvila.com/reviews/best-pellet-grill/); [Taste of Home](https://www.tasteofhome.com/collection/best-pellet-grills/); [Outdoor Life](https://www.outdoorlife.com/gear/best-grills/)).

## The council split on only two picks, which proves less than it seems

The picks were built in three stages. Researchers compiled four notes files on 17 November 2026 covering expert reviews, lineups and prices, performance by use case, and ownership factors. Three council seats (A, B and C), each a different AI model, then answered the question separately, in fresh contexts, without seeing one another's answers and reading only those four files. A fourth model acted as judge: it compared the three answers, re-checked disputed facts against live maker and retailer pages, and wrote the verdict whose final table this report adopts.

Two limits apply to the agreement. First, **all three seats read the same evidence**, and that evidence leans heavily on one outlet, Smoked BBQ Source. Unanimity therefore means "the available evidence points one way," not "three independent investigations agree." Second, the seats are different models from one model family and can share blind spots, so their agreement is worth more than three runs of one model and less than agreement between unrelated ones.

The budget split was the sharper of the two disagreements. Seats A and B backed the Pit Boss 850 DX on its test record, features and sub-$700 regular price. Seat C backed the Weber Smoque because its price was confirmed that day, it tested as the most even-heating grill in the band and it carries a five-year warranty, while the notes gave no current price for the Pit Boss. The judge kept the Pit Boss because it qualifies at its regular price, and ruled the Smoque the better buy for as long as it is $599 for a buyer who values evenness, warranty and app over features.

The portable split came down to a fact, not a judgment. Seat B chose the Traeger Tailgater for tailgating only because it could not confirm that the Grilla Chimp 2.0 was still being sold. The judge's check showed it was, so the pick went to the Chimp 2.0, which the only dedicated portable test preferred, with the Tailgater kept as the fallback. The capacity runner-ups were compatible answers to different needs, so both stayed.

It also confirmed MAK's closure, the recteq X-Fire Pro price at four retailers, Weber's Searwood warranty terms, and the burn-pot description on the Lone Star 20x36 page.

## Six claims stayed unverified and the best-known test labs went unread

The judge marked six claims as unverified after its checks. Each one bears on a pick.

Four softer points sit alongside these. The recteq X-Fire Pro's 1,250°F maximum is the maker's number with no independent measurement. The Lone Star's smoke superlative comes from one reviewer who tested a different size. Whether Pit Boss's five-year warranty covers the 850 DX is unconfirmed. And the Weber and Grilla sale prices show no end date.

The larger gap is what nobody could read. Serious Eats, Wirecutter, CNET, Food & Wine, Good Housekeeping, Popular Mechanics, Wired and Bon Appétit blocked the research tools, so their picks are absent from this report entirely. Consumer Reports is paywalled; a search summary names its three top-scoring pellet grills as the Dyna-Glo Signature Series, Expert Grill Atlas and Traeger Ironwood 650, two of which no other outlet in the research mentions ([Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/home-garden/grills/best-pellet-grills-of-the-year-a4395560003)). AmazingRibs' full reviews, The Barbecue Lab's website and Hey Grill Hey returned errors, so they appear only through forum posts, a YouTube video and search summaries. recteq's own site refused every request during research. Reddit was unreachable, which leaves long-term reliability resting on Better Business Bureau records, warranty text and forum anecdotes.

The source base also has a tilt. Smoked BBQ Source is the most-cited hands-on tester here; it is funded by affiliate links, and its Weber, Traeger and Yoder review units were supplied free by the makers ([Searwood review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=57742); [Woodridge Pro review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/traeger-woodridge-pro-review/); [YS640s review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/yoder-ys640s-review/)). The picks it drives are most believable where an unrelated source reports the same thing, as with the Searwood's uneven grate and the Woodridge Pro's weak searing.

## Conclusion: five checks before you pay

Confidence in these picks runs opposite to price. The $899 Searwood carries the highest rating in the set at 0.7, and the $2,945 Lone Star Grillz carries the two lowest at 0.3 and 0.4, because a mass-market grill gets tested and measured by several outlets while a small maker's smoker gets one review and some forum threads. The picks also divide into two camps that do not overlap: direct-flame grills that sear, and heavy or insulated smokers that top out at 450 to 600°F or cannot sear at all. No single grill wins searing, smoke flavor and cold weather together, and at today's sale prices a Searwood 600 and a Mammoth together cost about $1,740, less than either heavy-steel grill alone. Several of the picks are priced on sales with no stated end date, so the budget ranking in particular can change within days.
