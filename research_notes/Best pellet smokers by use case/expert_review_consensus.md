## 1. Which models do the major outlets name in each category in their most recent roundups? (outlet x category x model matrix)

### Takeaway
Among the roundups I could actually read, the Weber Searwood 600 (and its 36" sibling, Searwood XL 600) is the most-awarded pellet grill of 2026 (Best Overall at Smoked BBQ Source and Taste of Home, Best for Searing at Bob Vila), with the Traeger Woodridge Pro the repeat "best for beginners" pick, the Yoder YS640s the repeat premium pick, a Pit Boss model the repeat budget pick, and the Traeger Tailgater the repeat (but poorly performing) portable pick. Bob Vila's Best Overall (Traeger Westwood XL) and Consumer Reports' top three are outliers.

## 2. What quantitative test results exist (temperature accuracy/variance, preheat, max temperature, smoke flavor scores, pellet consumption, lid-open recovery, sear tests)?

### Takeaway
Hard numbers are sparse and come mostly from Smoked BBQ Source, Reviewed (2024), GearJunkie, The Barbecue Lab and one AmazingRibs tester comment; the Searwood 600 has the best-documented data (600°F in 13 minutes; controller within 5°F, but 30–50°F zone differences), while the Woodridge Pro's documented weakness is high heat (grates under 300°F at a 500°F setting; 480°F actual at a 500°F set point). No outlet I could read publishes blind-taste smoke scores, measured smoke-ring depth, or timed lid-open recovery.

### Cited Findings

**Weber Searwood 600 / XL 600**
- Preheat: "The grill took 13 minutes to reach 600°F"; set to 250°F it reached 225°F in about 6 minutes and was fully preheated in about 9 minutes. — [SBS Searwood review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=57742)
- Controller stability: "The controller on the Weber never changed by more than 5°F." — [SBS Searwood review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=57742)
- Grate evenness: ThermoWorks Signals showed "around 50°F difference between the front and back," largest in the back-left corner; a steak charred darker on the hotter right side. — [SBS Searwood review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=57742)
- Second measurement of unevenness: "There was a 30 to 50° from left to right temperature difference." — [SBS top-5 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-ZPJ4veZVY)
- Sear: "the searing ability on the Searwood is the best that I've ever tested"; held temperature with the lid open in direct/manual mode. — [SBS top-5 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-ZPJ4veZVY)
- Smoke output (subjective, judged from exhaust): "the Searwood produces more smoke than the Woodwind Pro." — [SBS top-5 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-ZPJ4veZVY)
- Maintenance interval: went "5 or 6 cooks without touching" the ash catch; assembly about 1.5 hours. — [SBS Searwood review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=57742)
- Bob Vila sub-scores (Searwood XL 600): ease of use 4.7, temperature control 4.7, cooking performance 4.9, value 4.5; reached 600°F with "excellent searing" and fast heat recovery (no time given). — [Bob Vila](https://www.bobvila.com/reviews/best-pellet-grill/)
- Engadget: "preheats the device in 15 minutes or less"; score 80/100; temperature range 180–600°F. — [Engadget Searwood review](https://www.engadget.com/home/kitchen-tech/weber-searwood-review-second-times-the-charm-153037440.html)

### Inferences
- The Searwood's recurring complaints (wheels, uneven zones, shelves extra) are convenience/consistency issues, whereas the SmokeFire's were safety/reliability issues (jams, grease fires); no accessible 2026 tester reports the SmokeFire-type failures on the Searwood after 6+ months of use.
- Controller/electronics are the most commonly reported hardware fault on Traeger's 2025–2026 lines (Woodridge display failure; Timberline shutdown; ambient-probe inaccuracy), though each is a single-unit report.
- For the portable use case the "winner" carries the most negative hands-on evidence of any consensus pick, so the Tailgater should be presented as a default/availability pick, not a test-proven one.

### Gaps
- No multi-year durability data for the Searwood (launched 2024), Woodridge line (launched Jan 2025) or Westwood (2026) exists in accessible sources; the longest reports are ~1 year (Taste of Home Woodridge Pro; GearJunkie Woodridge) and ~6 months (SBS Searwood).
- No accessible source gives failure rates, warranty-claim statistics, or Consumer Reports brand-reliability/owner-satisfaction figures for pellet grills.
- Yoder long-term negatives beyond weight/price were not found; I did not read The Barbecue Lab's June 2026 YS640s review.

## 6. Which outlets actually buy and test units hands-on versus aggregate specs?

### Takeaway
None of the accessible roundups states that it buys all its test units; the specialist sites test hands-on but largely on manufacturer-supplied grills (disclosed per review), and several high-ranking "2026 best pellet grill" pages are spec/affiliate aggregators with no measurements. The outlets most likely to buy at retail (Consumer Reports, Wirecutter, GearLab) were inaccessible or have no pellet roundup.

### Cited Findings
- Smoked BBQ Source — hands-on, long-duration (3–6 months per grill), 39+ pellet grills cooked on; individual reviews disclose free units: "Weber sent me the smaller 600 model to test out" and "Traeger sent me this grill for free in exchange for an honest review"; affiliate links. Its video presenter restricts rankings to "actual grills that I have ran." — [SBS Searwood review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=57742); [SBS Woodridge Pro review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/traeger-woodridge-pro-review/); [SBS top-5 video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-ZPJ4veZVY)
- Taste of Home — hands-on product-testing team; "secured 12 grills from the most popular brands" (purchase vs. supplied not stated); tests originally run Aug 2024 with long-term testers checking in every three months; Wi-Fi was connected indoors with grills not running, so most connected features were not tested. — [Taste of Home](https://www.tasteofhome.com/collection/best-pellet-grills/)
- Bob Vila — hands-on (smoked pork, burgers, chicken, seared steaks, monitored temperature) over 2 weeks; claims 14 tested but methodology says "hands-on tested seven" and only seven are named; unit sourcing not stated; commerce/affiliate page. — [Bob Vila](https://www.bobvila.com/reviews/best-pellet-grill/)

### Inferences
- Reliability tiers for weighting: Tier 1 (current + hands-on + some instrumentation): Smoked BBQ Source, The Barbecue Lab, AmazingRibs (partial access), Taste of Home. Tier 2 (hands-on but thin data, inconsistencies, or single-model): Bob Vila, GearJunkie, Engadget (2025). Tier 3 (hands-on but stale): Reviewed (2024), Outdoor Life (2024), The Grilling Dad. Tier 4 (do not use as evidence): BBQ Report, Finding Dulcinea, knowledgelib.io, SpruceRank, smarthomeexplorer, and the many "10 Best Pellet Grills (Month 2026)" pages on unrelated domains that dominated search results.
- Because the best-documented test evidence comes from manufacturer-supplied units reviewed by affiliate-funded sites, agreement between those sites and non-affiliate signals (AmazingRibs tester measurements, owner reports of the same weaknesses) is more persuasive than any single award.
- Search results for this category are heavily polluted by rolling-date SEO pages; a headline "2026" does not indicate a 2026 test (Outdoor Life and Reviewed carry 2026 footers on 2024 content).

### Gaps
- Could not verify purchase policies or methodologies for Consumer Reports, Wirecutter, Serious Eats, CNET, Food & Wine, Good Housekeeping Institute or Popular Mechanics (inaccessible).
- Smoked BBQ Source's roundup page itself does not state how units are sourced; the disclosure is only on individual review pages. An old reader-comment reply on that page says "No one in our team has ever done any hands-on tests with the Grilla," which predates the Searwood's 2024 launch and conflicts with the current page's Grilla Mammoth/Alpha Connect test notes; I could not confirm when Grilla testing began. — [Smoked BBQ Source](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/best-pellet-smokers/)
- Bob Vila's seven unnamed "tested" models (of 14 claimed) are not identified, so it is unknown which competitors the Westwood XL actually beat.
