## 2. Reasoning by use case

**1. Best overall: Weber Searwood 600**
- Why: Smoked BBQ Source (Nov 2026) and Taste of Home (Sep 2026) both rank it Best Overall. It has the best measured data in the notes: 600°F in 13 min, controller within 5°F, and about 585°F at the grate per AmazingRibs. Lowe's shows 4.6/5 from 1,820 reviews.
- Not the Traeger Westwood XL or Woodridge Pro: Bob Vila's Westwood XL pick is a single-outlet outlier capped at 450°F. The Woodridge Pro cannot sear (grates under 300°F at a 500°F setting) and has no lid gasket.
- Against my pick: grate zones differ by 30-50°F. SBS found the barbecue "a tad drier." Wheels are flimsy and shelves cost extra. There are two owner "no sear" reports. Durability data covers 6 months at most.
- Don't buy if you want a $700 grill, want a set-and-forget app with remote start (Weber has none), or want a dedicated competition-grade smoker.

**2. Best budget: Pit Boss 850 DX**
- Why: SBS "Best Budget." It has PID control, Wi-Fi and a 21 lb hopper, and held steady on 8+ hour cooks. Pit Boss gives up to 5 years of warranty including electronics.
- Why not the Smoque: it is the evenest tested and has a stable parent, but SBS says its smoke is "not as deep as we would want," it has no sear, and its wheels are flimsy.
- Against: the price is unverified. The app took 45 minutes to set up, there is a left-right hot spot, and owner reports of controller and igniter faults are common. It is a Lowe's exclusive.
- Don't buy if you hate app friction, or if the 850 DX is out of stock (take the Smoque at $599 or the Pit Boss FB2 850 at $599.99).

**3. Searing: Searwood 600**
- Why: it is the only grill with measured grate temperature and whole-grate exposure. SBS says it is "the best that I've ever tested," and Bob Vila's searing pick is the XL.
- Why not the X-Fire Pro 825: it reads 800-1,200°F on its dial settings (not measured peaks), and Engadget scored it 84/100. But it has two 10 lb hoppers, no smoke-boost mode, and wobbled on a cold windy day. Its price comes from snippets.
- Against: the crust is below steakhouse level, and the right side runs hotter.
- Don't buy if you need an 800°F+ crust (look at the X-Fire Pro or add a propane sear station).

**4. Large capacity: Grilla Mammoth, with sub-cases**
- Sub-cases: crowd smoking on a budget is the Mammoth ($999) or the Pit Boss Sportsman 5-Series vertical ($649.99 on Pit Boss's site). A horizontal that also grills is the Searwood XL 600, but its main grate is only 630 sq in. Cash-no-object is the YS1500S.
- Why: it is SBS's top vertical and "Best Large" pick, and it is double-walled.
- Against: it is smoker-only. The fan is loud, it is hard to move, and the app is flaky. There is no rack-to-rack temperature data for any vertical. The "8 butts / 10 briskets" claim is implausible, and the YS1500S brisket counts are dealer claims.
- Don't buy if you also want to sear or crisp skin at high heat.

**5. Portable: no single winner**
- The Tailgater wins four general roundups by default. Hands-on notes: lowest cooking score on Bob Vila's list (3.8), a tough time holding temperature, ash fires, a rusted tray, and "tipsy" legs.
- The only dedicated 8-grill test (Barbecue Lab) preferred the Chimp 2.0 for tailgating and the Ranger for camping. I did not pick the Chimp because the notes do not confirm it is currently sold.
- For no-mains use, the Trek (12V native, 150-550°F, Wi-Fi) fits, but the only hands-on evidence is for its predecessor (temperature swings, a board failure). GMG's app is rated 2.6.
- Against: the Tailgater has no Wi-Fi and is 60 lb. Don't buy a Ranger for capacity (176 sq in).

**6. Cold / windy: low confidence**
- No controlled cold-weather data exists for any current model. Wind matters more than cold, and the owner anecdotes are mostly 2022-23 and on older units.
- Why LSG: a 3/16" chamber, a reviewer in Wisconsin, a Buffalo owner at 20°F in high wind ("holds the heat very well"), and a lifetime chamber warranty.
- Against: heavy single-wall steel is not fuel-efficient in wind. One owner says the FireBoard controller gets "glitchy" in the cold. The lead time is 8-14 weeks, and the max air temperature is 450°F.
- The value route is any PID grill plus a manufacturer or welding blanket and a windbreak. Don't buy an insulated Traeger if you want to sear in winter.

**7. Smart / app: Woodridge Pro**
- Why: the largest rating base, a steady update cadence, recipe-to-grill automation, and wireless probes. The Pro adds the pellet sensor and Super Smoke.
- Why not Weber: Weber Connect is good for guided cooks (recent testers praise it), but it is a smaller base with weaker graphs and no remote start.
- Against: Traeger had a Q3 2026 revenue drop of 40%, about $400M of debt, a reverse split, and cloud-dependent control. It cut off old controllers in 2020 and had a 2022 outage. BBB complaints in 2026 include Woodridge Pro panel failures.
- Don't buy if app longevity matters over 10 years, or if you need to sear.

**8. Premium buy-once: Yoder YS640S**
- Why: SBS "Best High-End" ("buy once, cry once"). Yoder has a transferable 10-year chamber warranty and a 3-year controller warranty. It uses a standard FireBoard controller.
- Why not the LSG: it has the thicker steel and lifetime warranty, but the build wait is 8-14 weeks, and the notes carry less recent independent evidence on it.
- MAK has closed, with no word on warranty or parts, so it is excluded.
- Against: the warranty excludes paint and rust, and the controller is covered for 3 years. It is 335-505 lb with no pellet dump, and it burns about 3x the pellets of a MAK, per one owner. Dealer prices vary.
- Don't buy in a humid or coastal area without a cover and touch-up paint (or consider an all-stainless LSG at $6,795).

**9. Smoke flavor: Lone Star Grillz 20x36 (split by budget)**
- Why: the reviewer called its smoke "unrivaled" and credits the mixed hot/smoldering burn pot. Owners agree, though one thread asks "are they really that smoky."
- The Yoder is a credible tie ("noticeably smokier than most," Oct 2026).
- Under $1,000, the Searwood has strong smoke per SBS and AmazingRibs. The Woodwind Pro Smoke Box was the mechanism-based favorite, but it is reported discontinued and sold out.
- Against: no blind multi-grill taste test exists, and the LSG review dates from 2024. Don't buy if you need fast delivery or high-heat grilling.

## 3. Claims my picks hinge on that are unverified or weakly sourced

- Pit Boss 850 DX current price and availability: not confirmed (a Lowe's exclusive with only an expired deal and a Dec 2025 range). Affects the budget pick.
- Searwood $899 is an "early Black Friday" price with no end date. Weber's warranty, hopper size and country of manufacture were not on its pages. Affects overall and searing.
- The 585°F grate reading is a snippet of the AmazingRibs review. Two owner reports contradict the sear. Affects searing.
- recteq X-Fire Pro 825 and Bullseye Deluxe: no instrumented data, recteq.com was blocked, and the prices are snippets. Affects the searing runner-up.
- Camp Chef Woodwind Pro "discontinued" rests on one SBS note. Camp Chef's site shows it sold out and dealer-only. Affects smoke flavor.
- Grilla Chimp 2.0 current availability and price are unconfirmed, and the Mammoth's $999 price is from SBS, not Grilla's page. Affects portable and capacity.
- The current GMG Trek Prime 2.0 has no hands-on review, and its 12V claim is the manufacturer's. Affects portable.
- Cold-weather picks rest on 2022-23 anecdotes and construction specs. There is no measured pellet use at a stated low temperature for any current model.
- LSG's smoke superlative comes from one 2024 review (updated May 2025) plus forum anecdotes. Affects smoke flavor and cold weather.
- The Traeger warranty length conflicts (3 years on Traeger's page vs 10 on retailer listings), and Traeger's Q3 figures are from its 8-K but long-term solvency is an inference. Affects smart/app.
- Capacity counts (briskets, butts) for the YS1500S, LSG and Mammoth are dealer or manufacturer claims.
- Consumer Reports (paywalled), Wirecutter, Serious Eats and CNET were not readable, so their picks are absent.

## 4. Where I expect reasonable people to disagree with me

- Portable: many will pick the Grilla Chimp 2.0 or Traeger Ranger on the Barbecue Lab test. I chose the Tailgater on availability, with weak enthusiasm.
- Budget: some will say Weber Smoque, or the $549.99 Traeger Pro 22 (legacy), over a Pit Boss with a poor app and anecdotal faults, especially if the 850 DX price is not near $500.
- Searing: recteq fans will argue the X-Fire Pro or Bullseye beat the Searwood on heat. I weighted measured data over claims.
- Smoke flavor and premium: the Yoder has better recent evidence and shorter lead times, so some will make it the smoke pick and the LSG the runner-up. Others will point to the Woodwind Pro if stock turns up.
- Smart/app: some will pick Weber because Traeger's finances are shaky, or the Timberline for the full feature set at $3,499.99.
- Large capacity: others will say a vertical is not a "grill" and pick a horizontal (Searwood XL, Pit Boss Pro Series 1600, YS1500S).

## 5. Overall confidence

**0.5.** The overall, searing, smart and premium picks have decent current hands-on support, but the cold-weather, portable, smoke-flavor and capacity picks rest on thin, subjective or claim-level evidence, and several prices are unconfirmed.
