**2. Reasoning by use case**

**1. Best overall: Searwood 600**
- Why: Best Overall at Smoked BBQ Source (Fall 2026, 4.5/5) and Taste of Home (Sep 2026), and #1 in SBS's late-2025 ranking. SBS measured 600°F in 13 minutes with the controller within 5°F.
- Why not the obvious alternative: Bob Vila's Best Overall, the Traeger Westwood XL, tops out at 450°F and needed a skillet for steaks. It is a single-outlet pick.
- Strongest case against: two measurements show a 30-50°F left-to-right spread, testers call the barbecue drier, and the wheels are flimsy. Weber's page does not state warranty terms.
- Should not buy: anyone who wants steakhouse-level searing, or anyone who needs to read the warranty before purchase.

**2. Best budget: Weber Smoque**
- Why: Tested by SBS (May 2026); reached 275°F in about 10 minutes with near-identical left-to-right readings. It has a 5-year limited warranty and a stable corporate parent (Weber Blackstone).
- Why not Pit Boss 850 DX: it is the consensus budget pick (SBS top-five and budget roundup), but the notes give no current US price. The only price is an expired $449 deal.
- Strongest case against: the Smoque is under $700 only on the early sale; its regular price is $799. SBS's author would still pick the Bullseye or a Pit Boss first, and the Smoque has no direct sear, thin steel and "flimsy plastic wheels."
- Should not buy: anyone who wants to sear, or anyone who will pay full price.

**3. Searing: Searwood 600**
- Why: The only pellet grill in the notes with instrumented grate data near steak-cooking heat: about 585°F at the grate (AmazingRibs, snippet) with whole-surface direct flame. SBS calls its searing "the best that I've ever tested."
- Why not X-Fire Pro 825: its 225-1,250°F range and purpose-built dual fire pot make it the spec leader. But the notes give only Engadget's setting bands (Max 800-1,200°F) and no measured grate temperature. Measured beats manufacturer.
- Strongest case against: owners report no sear at 500°F, the right side runs hotter, and no pellet grill in the notes reaches a true steakhouse crust.
- Should not buy: anyone who wants 800°F+ searing. A propane sear station does that better, but the Camp Chef Sidekick is out of stock and the Woodwind Pro is discontinued.

**4. Large capacity: Grilla Mammoth**
- Why: 1,660 sq in, expandable to 3,300, with a 40 lb hopper. SBS ran two pork butts for 22 hours with the hopper still half full, and it is SBS's Best Large and Best Overall vertical.
- Why not Yoder YS1500s: the largest horizontal (dealer claims of 6-8 packer briskets) at about $5,195, but every capacity figure is a dealer claim.
- Strongest case against: it is a smoker only (no searing), the fan is loud, chunk loading is awkward, the unit has no handle, and Grilla's "8 pork butts or 10 briskets" for five racks looks implausible.
- Should not buy: anyone who wants to grill burgers for a crowd, or anyone who needs to move the unit often.

**5. Portable: no single winner**
- General pick, Traeger Ranger: the only portable with measured data in two tests (OutdoorGearLab: 176 sq in, 54 lb, heating and temperature control both 8/10). It is $479.99 on traeger.com. The Barbecue Lab saw a stuttering start and temperature swings.
- No-mains camping or RV: GMG Trek Prime 2.0. Its native 12V operation and 165 W ceiling avoid the 300-500 W AC surge of other units. But only the predecessor was tested (temperature swings and a board failure), and GMG's app averages 2.6.
- Tailgating: Grilla Chimp 2.0, the Barbecue Lab's pick. It has 460 sq in but weighs 90 lb, is AC-only without the optional power station, and its price is unverified.
- Strongest case against the Ranger: $480 for 176 sq in, 54 lb and 120V only. Do not buy the Traeger Tailgater: it has the weakest tested performance, an owner-reported ash fire and a rusted drip tray, even though it is the default pick in most roundups.

**6. Cold and windy: Lone Star Grillz 20x36**
- Why: 3/16-inch steel and a FireBoard PID controller. Owners report it "holds the heat very well" at 20°F with high winds in Buffalo, NY. An SBS reviewer says it held temperature through a Wisconsin winter.
- Why not Traeger Ironwood: it is fully insulated, dual-wall, and $1,799.99, so it is the cheaper insulated option. But its cold evidence is manufacturer copy and a GearJunkie snippet, and the older Ironwood 885 drew cold-weather complaints.
- Strongest case against: no controlled cold test exists for any current model. A Kansas Yoder owner saw pellet use rise sharply in wind until he added a welding blanket, so steel mass alone may not solve wind loss. The LSG has an 8-14 week lead time.
- Should not buy: anyone who needs the grill quickly, or anyone who expects high-heat cooks below freezing (every design in the notes struggles there).

**7. Smart / app: Woodridge Pro**
- Why: Traeger's app is 4.8 from about 312K Apple ratings, and SBS calls WiFIRE "the benchmark for reliability and ease of use." The Woodridge Pro adds Super Smoke, a pellet sensor and Keep Warm.
- Why not Searwood: Weber Connect guides cooks well (Engadget praised it), but its iOS base is about 2.8K ratings, and its graphing and cook history are weaker.
- Strongest case against: the 4.8 is inflated by rating prompts, the app depends on Traeger's cloud (with a 2020 cut-off of older controllers), revenue fell 40% in Q3 2026, and the warranty is 3 years on traeger.com but 10 years at retailers.
- Should not buy: anyone who wants to sear, or anyone who is worried about Traeger's support continuity.

**8. Premium / buy once: Yoder YS640S**
- Why: 10-gauge chamber with a 10-year burn-through warranty and 3 years on the controller. SBS named it Best High-End ("buy once, cry once"), and its reviewer used the standard cart for years without a reported failure. It is made in Kansas.
- Why not Lone Star Grillz: LSG offers the stronger paper warranty (lifetime chamber and structural steel) and thicker steel at $2,945. But the notes have no multi-year reviewer evidence for it, and the lead time is 8-14 weeks. Either choice is defensible; I take Yoder for the stronger evidence.
- Strongest case against: Yoder's warranty excludes paint and rust, so coastal or humid use is a real risk. The unit is 335 lb and has no pellet dump. SBS's unit was supplied free by Yoder, and one reader reported a controller-board failure in 2022.
- Should not buy: anyone in a humid or coastal climate without a cover, or anyone who needs to move the grill often.

**9. Smoke flavor: Lone Star Grillz 20x42**
- Why: SBS's reviewer calls its bark and smoke "unrivaled," and credits a burn pot that mixes hot-burning and smoldering pellets. Owners make similar claims.
- Why not the Woodwind Pro: it was the mechanism-based choice at mid-price, but SBS reported it discontinued on 7 Oct 2026 (a single source), so only leftover stock remains.
- Strongest case against: no blind multi-grill test exists. The reviewer tested only the 20x42, and the notes do not confirm that the $2,945 20x36 has the same burn pot. The Yoder YS640S is described as "noticeably smokier than most" by its own reviewer, and the closest blind test was a briquette-versus-pellet comparison with confounds.
- Should not buy: anyone who wants low maintenance or a light, mobile grill. The 20x42 weighs 445 lb and tops out at 450°F air temperature.

**3. Claims my picks hinge on that are unverified or weakly sourced in the notes**

- Searwood 600 price ($999 regular, $899 sale): the $899 figure from SBS and Walmart is a sale or undated price; the sale end is not shown. Affects overall, searing and budget comparisons.
- Searwood 600 grate heat of about 585°F: the AmazingRibs figure is from a search snippet. Owner reports of no sear at 500°F contradict it. Affects searing.
- Searwood 30-50°F left-to-right spread: SBS reports it twice, while AmazingRibs describes only a slight drop-off. Affects searing and overall.
- Searwood warranty: not on Weber's product page. Affects overall (buy-once and support risk).
- Smoque at $599: a sale price tied to early Black Friday. Affects budget.
- Pit Boss 850 DX price: no current US price in the notes, and it is a Lowe's exclusive. Affects the budget runner-up.
- Grilla Mammoth at $999: sourced only from SBS and not confirmed on Grilla's site; SBS's max temperature is not stated. Affects large capacity.
- Ranger weight (54 lb measured vs 60 lb on a comparison page) and Chimp price ($719 video vs $649.99 forum post): conflicting. Affects portable.
- GMG Trek 12V operation: a manufacturer claim. The only hands-on test was of the predecessor, the Davy Crockett. Affects no-mains portable.
- Cold-weather performance of LSG and Yoder: owner anecdotes mostly from 2022-2023, and SBS says it has "no formal data." Affects cold and premium.
- LSG smoke superlative: one reviewer, tested on the 20x42 only, with no blind test. The 20x36's equivalence is unconfirmed. Affects smoke and the cold runner-up logic.
- Traeger warranty: 3 years on traeger.com's service page versus 10 years on Home Depot and P.C. Richard listings. Affects smart pick and overall runner-up.
- Traeger corporate risk: the Q3 2026 figures come from an SEC 8-K summary cited in the notes, and the Q2 2026 figures come from a summary the notes themselves flag "verify." Affects smart, overall runner-up and Ranger.
- Woodridge Pro price: traeger.com shows a "Sale Price" of $1,149.99 with no strikethrough, and an unlabeled $999.99 in the header. Affects smart pick.
- Yoder YS640S review: the unit was supplied free by Yoder. Affects premium.
- X-Fire Pro 825 1,250°F: a manufacturer figure; the Engadget bands are settings, not measured peaks. Affects the searing runner-up.
- Woodwind Pro discontinuation: a single source (SBS, 7 Oct 2026). Affects smoke, where it removes the mechanism-based pick.

**4. Where I expect reasonable people to disagree with me**

- Best overall: many buyers will take the Traeger Westwood XL (Bob Vila) or the Ironwood, and some will call the Searwood's 30-50°F spread disqualifying for a "best" grill.
- Searing: a buyer who reads specs will pick the X-Fire Pro 825. My view is that measured data beats a 1,250°F setting, but that position is cautious, not proven.
- Budget: the consensus pick is the Pit Boss 850 DX. Some will argue for it over the Smoque, which is under $700 only on sale and has no sear.
- Cold: many would spend the $2,945 differently and buy a PID grill plus a manufacturer blanket, on the view that wind, not steel, drives pellet use.
- Premium: Yoder versus Lone Star Grillz. Warranty-focused buyers will take LSG's lifetime chamber claim; evidence-focused buyers will take Yoder's reviewer use.
- Smoke: a blind test might reverse my pick. The superlative comes from one reviewer, and the Yoder is described as "noticeably smokier" by its own tester.

**5. Overall confidence**

0.4. Most picks rest on hands-on but affiliate-funded or single-reviewer evidence, with no controlled cold or smoke tests. Serious Eats, Wirecutter, Consumer Reports and AmazingRibs' full reviews were not accessible, several prices are sale-only or conflict between sources, and Traeger's warranty and finances are unresolved. The Searwood overall pick and the Weber budget sale are the best-supported calls.
