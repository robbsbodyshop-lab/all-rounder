## Key Question 1: What is each major brand's current lineup, positioning and price?

### Takeaway
Traeger's ladder is now Westwood ($700–800, new April 2026) → Woodridge ($900–1,800) → Ironwood ($1,800–2,000) → Timberline ($3,500–4,000), with Pro 22/34 and Ironwood 885 relegated to "Legacy"; Weber sells only Searwood and Smoque (no SmokeFire), both discounted $100–200 for early Black Friday. Pit Boss has reorganised around Carbon / FB2 / Sportsman / Navigator (connected) / Onyx plus many retailer exclusives, Camp Chef's site shows only four Woodwind models (two Pro models sold out), and several mid-market lines (Oklahoma Joe's Rider, Kamado Joe Pellet Joe) are empty or out of stock on their makers' own sites.

### Inferences
- Woodridge list prices are up 12.5% (base), 15% (Pro) and 12.5% (Elite) versus launch, which matches Traeger's "low double digits" tariff pricing; this is a calculation from the two cited sources, not a figure Traeger published per model.
- Traeger's warranty exposure is a judgement call for buyers: the company generated positive free cash flow in Q3 2026, holds $67.8M cash and is still guiding to positive full-year adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow, but it carries ~$400M of debt against shrinking revenue and needed a reverse split to stay NYSE-listed. Long (7–10 year) Traeger warranties depend on the company remaining a going concern; nothing in the filings fetched says it will not.
- Because Traeger.com no longer sells direct (per the Q3 2025 plan), prices on traeger.com function as reference/MAP prices and actual purchase happens at retailers; the page fetched showed no explicit add-to-cart control, consistent with that.
- Buyers of MAK (closed) and, to a lesser degree, Oklahoma Joe's Rider and Kamado Joe Pellet Joe (apparently end-of-line) face the highest parts/support uncertainty in this market.
