# Council verdict: best pellet smokers by use case (US, 17 Nov 2026)

## What kind of council this was

Three seats (A, B, C), each a different Claude model, answered the same question in separate fresh contexts without seeing each other. Each read the same four research-notes files in this folder and nothing else. A fourth model acted as judge, checked the disputed facts against live sources, and wrote this verdict.

Two limits on how much the agreement is worth:

- **Shared evidence.** All three seats read the same notes, and those notes lean heavily on one outlet (Smoked BBQ Source). Unanimity here means "the available evidence points one way", not "three independent investigations agree".
- **Shared lineage.** The seats are different models from one family, so they may share blind spots. Their agreement is stronger than three stances of one model, weaker than agreement between unrelated models.

The seats' full answers are in `council_seat_A.md`, `council_seat_B.md` and `council_seat_C.md`.

## Disagreements and how the judge resolved them

### Budget: Pit Boss 850 DX (seats A, B) vs Weber Smoque (seat C)

- **Strongest case for the 850 DX:** The one outlet that tested both ranks it the best budget grill. It has PID control, WiFi and 840 sq in, and its regular price ($649) is already under the budget ceiling, with 2026 sales at $449-$499.
- **Strongest case for the Smoque:** Its price is confirmed today ($599, early Black Friday; regular $799). It tested as the most even-heating grill in the price band, carries a 5-year warranty, and has a stable parent company and a better app. The 850 DX has a hot spot, a 45-minute app setup in testing, and common owner reports of controller and igniter faults.
- **Resolution:** Pit Boss 850 DX is the standing pick because it qualifies at its regular price and has the stronger test record for value. The Weber Smoque is the better buy for as long as it is $599, for a buyer who values evenness, warranty and app over features. It stops qualifying as "budget" at its regular $799. Neither sears well.

### Portable, tailgating sub-case: Grilla Chimp 2.0 (seats A, C) vs Traeger Tailgater (seat B)

- **Seat B's reason for the Tailgater** was that the notes did not confirm the Chimp 2.0 is still sold. The judge's check shows it is, at $719.99 list and $503.99 apparent sale price.
- **Evidence:** The only dedicated portable comparison (The Barbecue Lab, 8 grills) preferred the Chimp 2.0 for tailgating. The Tailgater wins general roundups by default but tested poorly: lowest cooking score in one roundup, trouble holding temperature, ash fires and a rusted tray.
- **Resolution:** Grilla Chimp 2.0 for tailgating, with the objection removed. Its costs are real: 90 lb and 120V only. The Traeger Tailgater stays as the lighter, cheaper, easy-to-find fallback.

### Large capacity runner-up

All seats pick the Grilla Mammoth for smoking for a crowd. They differ only on what to offer someone it does not fit, and the answers are compatible: Pit Boss Sportsman 5-Series vertical ($649.99) as the cheaper smoker-only option, and Yoder YS1500S ($5,195) for a horizontal that also grills.

**Overall confidence: 0.5.** The overall, searing, app and premium picks rest on consistent, current hands-on evidence. The cold-weather, smoke-flavor, portable and capacity picks rest on single reviewers, anecdotes or unmeasured claims.

### Judge's notes on the unanimous picks

- **Searwood 600 wins two categories for one reason:** it is the only mid-price pellet grill with direct flame across the whole grate and measured heat-up data. Its known weaknesses are a 30-50°F left-to-right spread, flimsy wheels and slightly drier barbecue. Durability evidence covers about a year at most.
- **The cold-weather pick is the weakest in the set.** All three seats chose it at 0.3-0.35 and all three said the value answer for most buyers is a blanket and a windbreak. Owner reports agree that wind matters more than cold. This should be presented as "best performer on thin evidence", not as a confident recommendation.
- **The app pick carries company risk.** Traeger's Q3 2026 revenue fell about 40%, it carries roughly $400M of notes, and its app depends on its cloud. The warranty question resolved in Traeger's favour (10 years on the Woodridge), but a buyer who plans to keep a grill for 10 years should weigh this.
- **Premium and smoke flavor are a two-horse race.** Yoder YS640S and Lone Star Grillz 20x36 swap places between the two categories. Yoder has the more recent independent review and shorter lead time; Lone Star has thicker steel and a lifetime chamber warranty. Either is defensible for either category.
- **Excluded on availability:** MAK (closed) and the Camp Chef Woodwind Pro (sold out at the maker, reported discontinued). The Woodwind Pro would have been the mid-price smoke-flavor pick; a buyer who finds dealer stock could still consider it, knowing parts support is uncertain.

## Points that still need a human

1. **Today's Pit Boss 850 DX price**, which decides the budget pick against the Smoque. Check lowes.com with a local store selected.
2. **Sale end dates.** Weber's early Black Friday prices and Grilla's lower prices show no end date. Camp Chef's banner says its sale ends 4 December.
3. **Major outlets not read.** Wirecutter, Serious Eats, Consumer Reports and the full AmazingRibs reviews blocked automated access. A reader with access should check whether any of them contradict these picks, especially Consumer Reports' pellet ratings.
4. **Owner sentiment on Reddit** (r/pelletgrills, r/Traeger, r/recteq) was also unreachable, so long-term reliability rests on BBB records, warranty text and forum anecdotes.
5. **Personal fit.** Which portable sub-case applies, and whether a cold-climate buyer would rather spend on a heavy grill or on a blanket, are the buyer's calls.
