## Searing: which pellet grills genuinely sear, measured grate temperatures, and how the approaches compare

### Takeaway
The independent measurements that exist show conventional indirect pellet grills do not sear (a Traeger Woodridge Pro set to 500°F had grates under 300°F), full-surface direct designs like the Weber Searwood get to roughly 585–600°F at the grate but unevenly, and slide-plate designs deliver a small hot spot rather than a searing surface. The only pellet-fired designs credibly aimed at a steakhouse crust are recteq's very-high-heat grills (X-Fire Pro 825, Bullseye Deluxe), but their 1,000–1,250°F figures are manufacturer numbers that I could not find independently instrumented; a propane sear accessory (Camp Chef Sidekick) remains the most dependable route but is not pellet-fired.

### Cited Findings
**Baseline: why most pellet grills fail at searing**
- [M] Traeger Woodridge Pro (current generation, 2025 launch): "The grill grates stayed under 300°F with the grill set to 500°F," measured with an IR gun; the reviewer's summary is that it "won't sear." — [Smoked BBQ Source, Fall 2026 guide (published 27 Oct 2026, updated 11 Nov 2026)](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=802)
- AmazingRibs' test methodology notes a grill "may be able to hit 900°F air temperature but still not be able to make a proper sear if the meat doesn't get exposed to IR" (snippet) — i.e. air/setpoint temperature is not the same thing as radiant searing heat. — [AmazingRibs, how we test](https://amazingribs.com/how-we-test-grills-and-smokers/)
- Measurement caveat: an IR gun pointed through a grate may read the fire below rather than the grate. Forum example (a charcoal kettle setup, not a pellet grill): an IR thermometer pegged at its 1,022°F maximum and the poster concluded "it was reading the coals directly (well below the grate)" (snippet). IR-gun grate numbers quoted by reviewers and marketers should be read with this in mind. — [AmazingRibs forum](https://pitmaster.amazingribs.com/forum/accessorize-your-pit/slow-n-sear/118804-max-temperature-for-slow-n-sear)

### Inferences
- Ranking of approaches by evidence quality for a real crust: (1) propane sear accessory or separate burner — highest heat claim with the least dependence on the pellet fire, but it is gas, costs about $300 extra on Camp Chef, and the host grill may be discontinued; (2) recteq very-high-heat pellet designs (X-Fire Pro 825, Bullseye Deluxe) — the only pellet-fired grills engineered for 800°F+; reviewer endorsement exists but no instrumented grate map was found; (3) Weber Searwood — the best-documented option, with roughly 585°F measured at the grate and whole-surface direct exposure, enough for good browning but below steakhouse-broiler heat, with a hotter right side; (4) slide-plate designs (Pit Boss, older Camp Chef) — a small flame window; the one measurement found was about 480°F; (5) indirect pellet grills (Traeger Woodridge/Ironwood/Timberline) — not searing tools without a pan or GrillGrates.
- What each gives up: Searwood — some evenness and (per owners) patience on preheat; X-Fire Pro — hopper capacity (2 x 10 lb), smoke-enhancing modes, and stability in cold wind; Bullseye — it is a small (380 sq in class) grill that is not primarily a low-and-slow smoker (capacity inferred from model name; not verified); Yoder with GrillGrates — sear zone limited to the area above the firebox; Sidekick — pellet purity and price.
- The Pit Boss "1,000°F" figure should be treated as unverified marketing; the only numbers with a method attached for slide-plate designs are in the 400–500°F range.

### Gaps
- No instrumented grate-temperature measurement was found for the recteq X-Fire Pro 825, Bullseye Deluxe (reading exists at Hey Grill Hey but was not retrievable), DualFire 1200, Yoder with GrillGrates, LSG direct grate, Pit Boss Flame Broiler (current models), or Camp Chef Sidekick.
- Serious Eats, Wirecutter, CNET, Outdoor Gear Lab and Consumer Reports results could not be retrieved (search did not surface Serious Eats/Wirecutter pages; Consumer Reports is paywalled). YouTube sear shootouts (Tom Horsman, Grill Sergeant, etc.) were not searched.
- Bullseye Deluxe current price and exact cooking area were not confirmed.
- Whether Camp Chef's Slide & Grill on the last Woodwind/Woodwind Pro generation improved on the ~480°F measured on the older SmokePro SG is unknown.
- Bob Vila's 2026 top pick is a "Traeger Westwood XL," described as strong on fast heating and searing (snippet, [Bob Vila](https://www.bobvila.com/reviews/best-pellet-grill/)); this appears to be a new 2026 Traeger model that I did not research — its sear capability and max temperature need checking.

## Smoke flavor: which designs produce the most smoke flavor and why; controlled comparisons; smoke tubes and pellets

### Takeaway
Reviewer and owner consensus puts three design families ahead of ordinary PID pellet grills for smoke flavor: grills that burn real wood chunks or charcoal alongside pellets (Camp Chef Woodwind Pro Smoke Box — now reported discontinued; Grilla Mammoth and Camp Chef XXL Pro verticals with chunk trays), heavy-steel grills with burn pots designed to smolder (Lone Star Grillz, Yoder YS640s), and to a lesser degree smoke-boost modes (Traeger Super Smoke, Weber SmokeBoost). The evidence is almost entirely subjective: I found no rigorous blind multi-grill taste test, and the two small tastings that exist point in opposite directions and are confounded.

### Cited Findings
**Wood-chunk / charcoal augmentation**
- [R] Camp Chef Woodwind Pro Smoke Box "brings the flavor profile closer to that of a charcoal or offset smoker" and is "an amazing feature that gives the Woodwind Pro the edge if your focus is on smoking"; the box is small — "a few small wood chunks or two to three pieces of lump charcoal." The Woodwind Pro also has smoke levels 1–10 and a fan mode. SBS's category winner for smoking over the Weber Searwood. — [Smoked BBQ Source, Weber vs Camp Chef (updated 6 May 2026)](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/weber-vs-camp-chef/)
- [R] The Barbecue Lab's multi-grill rib test: "the three ribs that had the most visible smoke ring and prominent smoke flavor were the Woodwind Pro, Weber, and GMG" (snippet; page not fetched, test date and exact Weber/GMG models not confirmed). — [The Barbecue Lab, best pellet grill](https://thebarbecuelab.com/best-pellet-grill/amp/)
- BBQ Report lists the Camp Chef Woodwind Pro WiFi 24 as "best for smoke flavor" (snippet; affiliate-style site). — [BBQ Report](https://bbqreport.com/?p=14169)
- [R] Camp Chef XXL Pro vertical (reviewed June 2023, updated May 2025): integrated smoke box is small and chunks burn fast — reviewer suggests adding more about every hour; reported a good smoke ring, good bark and smoke flavor. — [Smoked BBQ Source XXL Pro review](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/camp-chef-xxl-pro-review/)
- [R] Grilla Mammoth vertical: no-wrap pork butts had "amazing bark and smoke flavor, with a nice smoke ring"; "It's built like a tank, holds its smoke and is efficient burning pellets"; wood-chunk loading is described as awkward. — [Smoked BBQ Source, Fall 2026 guide](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=802); [Smoked BBQ Source vertical guide (12 Nov 2026)](https://www.smokedbbqsource.com/?p=24112)

**Smoke tubes and pellet choice**

### Inferences
- Mechanism explanation consistent with the cited reviews: PID controllers keep a small, hot, clean-burning fire, which is efficient but light on smoke; designs score higher when they deliberately add smoldering fuel (Smoke Box chunks/charcoal, LSG's mixed burn pot, vertical chunk trays) or run long cooks at lower temperatures with smoke-boost cycling. Heavy steel itself is credited by reviewers for heat stability rather than for smoke; the burn-pot design is what LSG's reviewer credits.
- For buyers who want the strongest smoke from a pellet-fed cooker as of Nov 2026, the best-supported new-purchase options are Lone Star Grillz (reviewer superlative plus owner reports; trade-offs: 8–14 week lead time, 445 lb, 450°F max air temperature) and the Yoder YS640s (trade-offs: price about 2.7x a Searwood per SBS, 335–505 lb, no pellet dump). The Woodwind Pro has the strongest mechanism-based case at mid-price but is reported discontinued.
- Smoke tubes and pellet species are low-cost levers, but the evidence that they close the gap with a chunk-burning design is anecdotal only.
- No source ranked non-PID "smoke setting" temperature swings against PID grills; the claim that wider swings produce more smoke is common lore that this research could not source.

### Gaps

## Cold-weather and windy-climate cooking: which models hold temperature best and why; measured data; northern owner reports

### Takeaway
I found no controlled test that logged pellet consumption or temperature hold for named models at low ambient temperature; the evidence is owner reports plus construction specs. Those reports consistently say wind matters more than cold, that well-built grills (heavy steel or double-wall) hold 225°F fine well below freezing with modest extra pellet use, that high-temperature cooking is where cold hurts most, and that a $30–$100 blanket narrows much of the gap for single-wall grills.

### Inferences
- Engineering logic supported by the reports: heat loss scales with wall conductance and wind; double-wall/insulated bodies (Traeger Ironwood/Timberline, Grilla Mammoth, recteq Staq) and thick steel with high thermal mass (Yoder 10-gauge, LSG 3/16-inch) both dampen swings, but mass alone does not cut steady-state loss in wind — which matches the Yoder owner's report of dramatically higher consumption in wind until a welding blanket was added.
- Rough efficiency comparison from numbers above (different ambients and methods, so indicative only): insulated Mammoth about 0.9 lb/hr (about 20 lb over 22 hours); Woodridge Pro about 1 lb/hr at 225°F; LSG claims 1.5 lb/hr at 225°F. Heavy single-wall steel is not inherently fuel-efficient; insulated designs are.
- For cold-climate buyers the best-supported "regardless of price" picks are heavy/insulated cookers with real cold-climate owner reports (LSG in Buffalo and Wisconsin; Yoder; Traeger's insulated lines in Minnesota/Alberta), and the best-value approach is a competent PID grill plus a manufacturer blanket or welding blanket and a windbreak (recteq RT-700 and GMG Daniel Boone owner reports).
- High-heat grilling in winter is the weak point for every design (MAK owner: heavy pellet use above 500°F below 45°F; older MAK struggling to reach 400°F; X-Fire Pro fluctuations), so searing-focused buyers in cold, windy locations should expect degraded max-temperature performance.

- Reddit (r/pelletgrills, r/Traeger, r/smoking) and PelletFan threads were not retrieved; owner evidence above is from AmazingRibs' Pitmaster Club, Traeger Forum and search snippets, mostly dated 2022–2023 and mostly on previous-generation models.
